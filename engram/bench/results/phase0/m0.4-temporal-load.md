# M0.4 Temporal load test at the retain activity shape (P-9, N71, N139)

Result in one paragraph: on this rig Temporal sustained **about 275 to 340 events/s**, with the history service at 25
to 40 % of its 2 CPUs and persistence p99 between 17 and 42 ms in the clean rungs. The first runs also saw 348
events/s in a 5 minute soak at p99 65 ms, and the re-run after the review (WAL archiving on, a larger history SQL
pool) passed at 275 events/s (p99 42 ms) and failed at 337 events/s (p99 97 ms). That is **about 5 % of the 5 800
events/s peak** the plan sizes for and below even the 580 events/s lower bound. The target is **not met**; this note
is the "lower peak the rig reached" of the exit criterion (PLAN.md section 10.2 M0.4). The rig was starved (host CPU
pressure 36 to 84 % in the re-run, load average 8 to 35 on 4 cores), so the number is a floor for this machine and
says little about Temporal: read "What the number says" before using it.

## The rig (stated plainly)

* Machine: one VM, 4 vCPU (Intel Xeon at 2.10 GHz, about 5 % steal), 15 GB RAM, Linux 6.18, Docker 29.8 on overlay2.
* Shared with the other workers of the build (a TLC sweep at about 2 cores, Go builds, Postgres test containers): the
  load average during these runs was 8 to 30 on 4 cores (the `uptime` lines of the logs). The numbers are what this
  contended machine gave, not what 4 dedicated cores would give.
* Temporal: `temporalio/server` 1.31.3, split services (frontend, history, matching, worker) in four containers,
  `numHistoryShards = 512`, configuration in `deploy/compose/temporal/`; the history container limited to 2 CPUs (the
  percentages below are of that).
* Persistence: `temporal-postgres`, PostgreSQL 16.15 (Alpine), the dev profile of `deploy/postgres/gucs.yaml`
  (`shared_buffers` 256 MB, `wal_compression = zstd`, `max_connections` 300), `fsync` and `synchronous_commit` on
  unless a table says off, visibility on the same Postgres, a local disk with about 1 ms fsync.
* Driver: `bench/temporal-load` (Go, SDK 1.45.0), one process on the same machine: the worker (16 workflow and 16
  activity pollers per queue) and the open-loop starter; four task queues `shard-1..4`; the payload codec on
  (AES-256-GCM on every payload, 8 namespaces).
* Every container uses `network_mode: host` (CONFLICTS.md #3), so the cluster talks over loopback.
* The ladders of the first run (the tables below up to "Upper bounds") ran with WAL archiving OFF: `ladder.sh` did not
  create the pgBackRest stanza, so Postgres kept its first-boot `archive_mode = off` (review m14). The re-run at the end
  of "Results" has archiving on, a larger history SQL pool (100 + 30 connections, was 60 + 20) and records the host's
  CPU pressure.

## Workload

`RetainShape` (`bench/temporal-load/shape.go`) replays the activity graph of `RetainDocument` (PLAN.md section 5.1.2):
four activities (`LoadItem`, `Chunk`, `SummarizeDocument`, `PlanChunks`), `MarkProgress` per wave of at most 32
chunks, five activities per chunk run as one coroutine per chunk (`ExtractChunk` 150 B + 1.8 KB in and 3.5 KB out,
`EmbedChunk`, `ResolveEntities`, `BuildLinks`, `CommitChunk` keys-only), then `FinalizeVersion` and `MarkOperation`.
Every result is at most 4 KiB (N59). A document is 8 chunks and the activities do no work. Measured: about 183 history
events per workflow, 23 per chunk (the plan assumes about 20), 49 activities. "Events/s" counts the history events of
completed workflows (history length at the last activity plus 7; `drill-seed` showed the true count is always one
higher, so the tables are 0.5 % low and later runs use plus 8). An online cell at 2.9 chunks/s is 67 events/s on this
shape; 370 events/s is 16 chunks/s.

## Method

`bench/temporal-load/ladder.sh` resets the stack (empty Temporal database), then offers workflow starts at a fixed
rate for 45 to 60 s per rung (open loop: a slow cluster does not slow the offer). Per rung: completed events/s,
workflow latency, the CPU of every container from its cgroup, and the persistence latency histogram of the history
service (`persistence_latency`, buckets 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100 ms, quantiles interpolated as Prometheus does). A
rung is **sustained** when completions keep up with the offer (in flight stays at a few), history CPU is below 70 % of
its limit and persistence p99 is below 50 ms. Column "wf/s" is the offered workflow starts per second; the first rung
of a ladder doubles as warm-up.

## What the number says

* Sustained at the p99 target: 1.5 to 1.75 workflows/s, which is 275 to 320 events/s (three of three rungs, p99 17, 19
  and 35 ms, history CPU 32 to 39 % of 2 CPUs). 2.0 workflows/s (333 to 369 events/s) passed once (p99 33 ms) and
  failed once (178 ms); the 5 minute soak at 1.9/s held 348 events/s with p99 65 ms; the re-run after the review
  passed at 1.5/s (275 events/s, p99 42 ms) and failed at 2.0/s (337 events/s, p99 97 ms). Recorded peak: about 340
  events/s, with p99 below 50 ms guaranteed only up to about 275 to 300. Beyond the knee the cluster collapses instead
  of plateauing: completions fall as the backlog, and with it the latency of `GetWorkflowExecution`, grows.
* Not a Temporal CPU limit, and not shown to be a Postgres limit: the history service used 0.5 to 0.9 cores of its 2
  and no throttling was seen, while the host reported 36 to 84 % CPU pressure (some task always waiting for a CPU) and
  a load average of 8 to 35 on 4 cores, so queueing for CPU on a starved machine explains the latency growth at least
  as well as any cost of the write path. What the logs show about the write path - one transaction per workflow task
  or activity completion (about 330 per second at the knee), and `INSERT INTO executions_visibility` at 8 to 9 ms
  against 0.3 ms for other statements - is a cost, not proof that it is the limit: with `synchronous_commit` off the
  throughput barely moved (up to 411 events/s), so the disk is not the limit either. An earlier reading of this note
  (the write path is the limit) is withdrawn; only a run on a quiet machine can separate the two.
* The codec is cheap: the driver process (worker, starter and AES-GCM) used 0.12 to 0.25 cores at 180 to 370 events/s.
  The run without the codec reached 528 events/s once, with p99 73 ms, which is inside the run-to-run noise of this
  machine (the same rung swung by more than that between two resets).
* 512 history shards is not what limits here (history at 40 % of its CPUs, no shard-lock waits among the slowest
  operations). This test cannot show whether 512 suffices at 5 800 events/s, because that rate was never reached.

## Proposed consequences (this milestone does not edit the plan)

* Exit criterion: not met as written. Per N139 the lower peak goes into PLAN.md section 9 and `RetainBackfill`
  concurrency is capped to it. Proposed text: "tested to about 300 events/s sustained (350 peak) on a 4-vCPU shared
  rig; `RetainBackfill` is capped to 300 events/s of Temporal history (about 13 chunks/s per cell) until the test is
  repeated on a Temporal host sized like the production class". The M0.4 report carries the CONFLICTS.md entry.
* Re-run on dedicated hardware (8 or more cores for Temporal and its Postgres, NVMe, nothing else running) before
  `numHistoryShards = 512` is frozen as final in the register: `LADDER=10,20,30,40 STEP=120s HISTORY_CPUS=4
  bench/temporal-load/ladder.sh`.
* If a dedicated host also stays near 400 events/s, then the write path is the limit and the levers are the visibility
  store (an Elasticsearch or OpenSearch visibility store is the Temporal-supported way to take the visibility inserts
  off the primary Postgres) and fewer, fatter activities, not the shard count. This test cannot say which case holds.

## Reproduce

```
LADDER=1,2,3,4,6 STEP=50s OUT=table.md bench/temporal-load/ladder.sh
LADDER=1,2,3,4,6 STEP=50s SYNC_COMMIT=off CODEC=false OUT=table.md bench/temporal-load/ladder.sh
```

The raw per-rung JSON lines are written next to `OUT` as `OUT.raw` (git-ignored under `bench/results/`).
